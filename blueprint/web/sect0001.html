Let \(T : E \to _l[\mathbb {R}] E\) be a symmetric linear endomorphism on an \(n\)-dimensional real inner product space \(E\). Then

\[ \operatorname {Tr}(T) = \sum _{i=1}^n \lambda _i(T), \qquad \det (I - T) = \prod _{i=1}^n (1 - \lambda _i(T)), \]

and if \(T\) is positive-semidefinite (\(\langle v, T v\rangle \ge 0\) for all \(v \in E\)), then \(\lambda _i(T) \ge 0\) and \(\det (T) \ge 0\).

Proof ▶

Spectral diagonalization in an orthonormal eigenbasis together with weighted AM–GM on \(\mathrm{Fin}\, n\).

For any symmetric positive-semidefinite endomorphism \(T : E \to _l[\mathbb {R}] E\) on an \(n\)-dimensional real inner product space (\(n \ge 1\)),

\[ \det (T) \le \left(\frac{\operatorname {Tr}(T)}{n}\right)^n. \]

When \(\operatorname {Tr}(H_1) \le 1\) and \(\operatorname {Tr}(H_2) \le 1\), any \(c \ge 0\) satisfies \(c^n \sqrt{\det H_1}\sqrt{\det H_2} \le (c/n)^n\).

Proof ▶

Applies product-power AM–GM to the non-negative eigenvalues \(\lambda _i(T)\).

For any linear operator \(A : E \to _l[\mathbb {R}] E\) with \(\| A v\| \le c \| v\| \), the Gram operator \(A^* A\) satisfies \(\det (A^* A) = (\det A)^2\), \(\operatorname {Tr}(A^* A) = \sum _i \| A e_i\| ^2 \le n c^2\), and \(|\det A| \le c^n\). Moreover, any symmetric positive-definite \(H\) (\(\det H {\gt} 0\)) admits a symmetric inverse square root \(D = H^{-1/2}\) satisfying \(D^2 H = \operatorname {id}_E\) and \(\det D = (\sqrt{\det H})^{-1}\).

Proof ▶

Spectral calculus on the orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A^* A\) and \(H\).

Let \(L, H_1, H_2 : E \to _l[\mathbb {R}] E\) on an \(n\)-dimensional real inner product space (\(n \ge 1\)) with \(H_1, H_2\) symmetric positive-semidefinite and \(c \ge 0\). If \(|\langle L u, w\rangle | \le c \sqrt{\langle u, H_1 u\rangle }\sqrt{\langle w, H_2 w\rangle }\) for all \(u, w \in E\), then

\[ |\det L| \le c^n \sqrt{\det H_1}\sqrt{\det H_2}. \]

If furthermore \(\operatorname {Tr}(H_1) \le 1\) and \(\operatorname {Tr}(H_2) \le 1\), then \(|\det L| \le (c/n)^n\).

Proof ▶

Normalizes \(L\) on the right and left by \(H_1^{-1/2}\) and \(H_2^{-1/2}\) when \(\det H_i {\gt} 0\), reducing to \(\| A\| \le c\), and handles the degenerate case \(\det H_i = 0\) via kernel vectors.

Definition 1.5 Exponential distance weights and critical exponent

For a metric-measure space \((X, d, \mu )\), scale \(s \in \mathbb {R}\), and basepoint \(x \in X\), the exponential weight is \(w_s(x, z) = e^{-s d(x, z)}\), the critical exponent (volume entropy) is

\[ h(X, \mu , x_0) = \inf \left\{ s \in \mathbb {R}\; \middle |\; \int _X e^{-s d(x_0, z)}\, d\mu (z) {\lt} \infty \right\} , \]

and the exponentially weighted measure is \(\mu _x^s = e^{-s d(x, \cdot )}\mu \).

For any \(s \ge 0\) and \(x, y, z \in X\), the triangle inequality gives \(e^{-s d(x,z)} \le e^{s d(x,y)} e^{-s d(y,z)}\). Consequently, \(z \mapsto e^{-s d(x,z)}\) is \(\mu \)-integrable if and only if \(z \mapsto e^{-s d(y,z)}\) is \(\mu \)-integrable, and \(h(X, \mu , x) = h(X, \mu , y)\) is independent of the basepoint.

Proof ▶

Mutual domination of the integrands by the constant factor \(e^{s d(x,y)}\).
